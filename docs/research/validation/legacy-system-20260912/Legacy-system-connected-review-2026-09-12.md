# The legacy system: a connected story carried through time

**Research reconstruction and experiment scope correction · 12 September 2026**

The connected proposition in this body of research is that people preserved an integrated practice of observation, calendar keeping, memory and performance; architecture and marks carried it after its practitioners disappeared; and a later reader can recover the account by bringing those surviving channels back into relation. In that proposition, HNC describes the relationship between the signal, the observing reader and retained information. The timing of a reading belongs inside the system being tested.

The earlier Maeshowe frequency calculation tested a fixed set of assignments. It did not test this complete proposition. Its numerical discrepancies and control results remain relevant to that calculation, but they neither establish nor exhaust the larger temporal hypothesis.

This review joins seven native research documents, three repository code/data sources, the earlier Maeshowe audit and selected primary sources. All 180 indexed native-document texts were searched for connecting passages; seven central native files were independently checked against both their Git blob identity and SHA-256. That is corpus discovery and source verification, not independent confirmation of every historical claim in every document. Full public-upload coverage and the broader experiment campaign remain unfinished.

## The account recovered from the connected research

*The Full Story* supplies the large arc: **origin → transmission → integration → fragmentation → dispersion → recovery**. In the author's account, an inherited body of knowledge travels with practitioners, enters other communities, survives institutional changes in separated forms, and becomes readable together again. *The Atlantic Substrate*, *From A to B* and *People of the Threshold* develop its proposed carriers, journeys, exchanges, calendar practices and threshold encounters. Their cross-cultural connections are the author's historical synthesis; resemblance and repeated motifs alone do not establish a single continuous population or transmission route.

*The Dormant Seed* gives Maeshowe a particular role within that arc: a durable archive whose inscriptions, spatial arrangement, cipher conventions and recurring solstice reference are proposed to preserve more than an isolated sentence. Chapter 2 explicitly extends the caller–seer relationship across historical time. The original deposit and the later reader's simultaneous cross-reference belong to one proposed act of transmission. This is why reducing “two twig” to a request to identify exactly two separate marks was too narrow.

Three linked meanings must be retained. Physical twig counts provide a group and a position. The manuscript interprets their interaction as a caller–response performance. It then extends that interaction to a carver and a reader separated by centuries. These are related layers of the author's model; the physical encoding does not by itself prove either performance or transmission history. The existing corrected reader also retains medieval 16-rune group sizes and explicit ordering, instead of silently applying the manuscript's generic three-groups-of-eight description to every inscription.

In *The Dormant Seed* §2.3, the joined reading has six movements:

| Movement | What the author's interpretation says | Function in the whole account |
|---|---|---|
| Authority | “I am the last qualified practitioner.” | Establishes who can leave the transmission. |
| Temporal depth | “This place is older than us.” | Places the carvers within a longer inheritance. |
| The system | The alphabet, cipher protocol and visual key belong together. | Supplies the proposed means of reading. |
| Treasure | “The treasure IS the system itself.” | Identifies knowledge as the thing being preserved. |
| Warning | “Do not enter alone.” | Introduces the manuscript's need for a complementary reader or voice. |
| Invitation | “The search is unfinished.” | Hands the continuation to a later reader. |

These are the author's explicit interpretive messages, not six independently verified literal translations or a recovered schedule of six seasonal recitations. Read together, they say: **a practitioner places inherited knowledge into a durable system, leaves the means and conditions of reading it, and invites a later reader to continue the transmission.** That is the connected account already present in the corpus.

The calendar-wheel report adds a proposed way of addressing the material by phase. HNC supplies a proposed language for recurring signals, observation and memory. Together they make the research question temporal: **does a declared light and calendar state select an ordered reading, and do successive readings form the proposed larger account?** A high score from one fixed mapping does not answer that question.

## What changes through the day, year and longer return

The following clocks interact but cannot be substituted for one another.

| Time layer | Connection recovered | What a defensible timed reading must retain |
|---|---|---|
| Within a day | Moving light, visibility, shadows and the position of a reader can change what can be seen. | Observation time, position of the light on the surface, observer position, orientation and occlusion. |
| Across the year | Maeshowe winter illumination, solstices, equinoxes and the corpus's calendar phases provide proposed reference events. | The complete annual visibility pattern, including times when the chamber receives no direct beam. A summer-solstice recitation cannot be assumed from winter alignment. |
| Reading frame | The report constructs a Samhain-based wheel and an equinox reading pole offset by 135°. | The rotation, direction, reference year, day-number convention and transformation between diagram and chamber. |
| Calendar month | The research brings Coligny, Ogham and a tree-calendar interpretation into relation. | Their separate source rules and intercalations. They are not interchangeable calendars. |
| Nineteen-year return | *People of the Threshold* and the broader narrative connect return, song and calendar renewal. | A declared cycle epoch, year within the cycle and actual lunar/solar phase. |
| Lunar nodal motion | *Ancient Convergence* also invokes the approximately 18.6-year lunar cycle. | A separate lunar-geometry variable if moonlight or standstills are tested. |
| Historical time | The archive/reader relationship spans the building, carving, preservation and recovery periods; the wheel report compares 200, 1153 and 2026 CE. | Epoch-specific sky and horizon models, restoration uncertainty, source dating and the later reader's accumulated context. |

There is a primary literary connection across several of these scales. Diodorus II.47 describes daily praise, a nineteen-year return, and night-time music and dance during the interval from the vernal equinox to the rising of the Pleiades. The seasonal performance is described in connection with that return; it should not automatically become an annually repeated Maeshowe timetable. The same passage reports Greeks visiting the Hyperboreans and leaving inscribed offerings. This restores a reverse-visit connection absent from the Herodotus passage checked earlier. Diodorus explicitly frames the account as legendary; it does not identify Maeshowe or provide its rune-to-date key. [Diodorus, II.47](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Diodorus_Siculus/2B%2A.html)

The nineteen-year astronomical relation is an approximate return of lunar phase against calendar dates: NASA gives 235 mean synodic months as 6,939.688 days and nineteen Gregorian years as 6,939.6075 days. The lunar node's approximately 18.6-year rotation is a different period. Neither statement supplies an exact repeated light patch on a particular wall. [NASA calendar explanation](https://eclipse.gsfc.nasa.gov/SEhelp/calendars.html), [NASA lunar orbit explanation](https://eclipse.gsfc.nasa.gov/SEhelp/moonorbit.html)

Coligny is physical evidence for a calendar spanning five years with a reconstruction of 62 months. Its connection to the corpus's nineteen-year story requires an explicit model; the bronze is not itself a supplied nineteen-year paragraph selector. [Ain heritage record](https://patrimoines.ain.fr/n/calendrier-et-dieu-gaulois-de-coligny/n%3A321)

## What the recovered material says at particular phases

The clearest explicit phase-to-content mapping located so far is in *Pattern Recognition Archaeology* §5. It projects the Antarctic candidate's reported first-light and last-light dates onto a constructed calendar wheel:

| Declared input in the paper | Recomputed wheel angle | Paper's selected content |
|---|---:|---|
| Year-day 322, labelled approximately 18 September | 317.589°, rounding to 317.6°; NW sector | T-NW, the treasure pointer |
| Year-day 144, labelled approximately 25 March | 142.027°, rounding to 142.0°; SE sector | Nr.20, the master-carver certification |

Both angles reproduce using `360 × day / 365`. This confirms the arithmetic of those declared inputs. It does not confirm the Antarctic sunrise dates, the wall inventory, or a sunlight path from the passage to the selected inscription.

There is also an indexing discrepancy. In a common-year interval beginning 1 November, 25 March is elapsed day 144 and 18 September is elapsed day 321. The paper's 322 for September uses the other numbering convention. Switching either endpoint by a day leaves its broad NW or SE sector unchanged, but prevents treating the quoted fine angle as an exact date observation. Leap-year treatment must also be declared.

The independent Maeshowe field work provides a real timing layer: Reijs describes winter light falling left of and below the central recess and models its changing duration around solstice. His 1996 simulation gives back-wall illumination from 28 to 17 minutes before sunset on solstice day; other dates have different windows, sometimes with a second appearance. He separates observed events from simulation and discusses the changed passage geometry. His measured reference-line azimuth of 219°40′ ±15′ also needs reconciliation with the calendar report's 232° passage value before a shared geometry is assumed. Those data can constrain a future light model, but they do not identify a sequence of paragraphs. [Reijs's measurements and light-path study](https://www.archaeocosmology.org/maeshowe/eng/scot10.htm)

**“Light at the base” must remain an event within this connected reading.** The recovered material does not yet specify a uniquely evidenced surface coordinate, visibility rule and paragraph binding for that event. The corpus text search found related light/time passages, but no unambiguous operational instruction completing that chain. The report therefore retains the event explicitly, without guessing that “base” means a rune stem, floor or calendar origin.

The missing connection is precise: **observed contact at the base → visible/active marks → paired twig candidates → source passages → their order at that time**. Once that is available, different readings can be compared through daily motion, seasonal changes and the nineteen-year cycle while retaining earlier context. Until then, the six-movement interpretation and two calendar projections are recoverable, but a complete timed recital is unresolved.

## What the existing experiments actually cover

The Maeshowe control experiment enumerated 120,960 conditional configurations. The original program's baseline was Γ=0.493163, with nine complete inscriptions and a 29.2832 Hz assigned master beat. It did not reproduce the manuscript's different baseline. Under the stated controls, 15.2778% of configurations reached at least the baseline gamma; removing imposed phi amplitude factors removed the baseline phi matches. Those are results about the selected mathematical construction. They are not a test of a changing passage sequence, and the gamma rank is not a probability of the historical account being true or false. The preserved [Maeshowe results](</C:/Users/samle/Documents/Codex/2026-09-05/k/outputs/Maeshowe-repaired-experiment-results-2026-09-12.md>) contain the receipts and limitations.

The newly recovered `CelticOghamDecoder.read()` has no date or light input. It selects records using fixed `decoded` flags and aggregates them. Its timestamp records when the output was created; the function does not read the months' `start_day` fields. Two pairs share starts: months 1/13 use 355, and months 2/3 use 383. Several starts exceed the documented day-of-year range. The file attributes its tree-calendar interpretation to Robert Graves; it is a distinct constructed layer. These facts mean that this program cannot currently answer which paragraph is active on a given day, at a solstice or in a particular cycle year. No missing calendar dates have been silently invented to repair it.

HNC's simulated stability results remain evidence for the particular tested recurrence and horizon. They do not validate the proposed ancient transmission or provide the missing time-to-text bindings. The Grand Unified Framework also uses β=0.25 as an echo gain with a ten-sample delay. That is a separately defined role from the instability parameter corrected to β=1 in the author's other recurrence. A global replacement of every β would damage the corpus's version distinctions.

## Reconfigured experiment: the complete reading is the target

The accompanying temporal specification treats a full ordered account as the main outcome. Individual marks and channels remain traceable because their relationships are what the integrated claim depends on.

1. Freeze the source versions, complete inscription inventory, competing readings, coordinate frame, calendar conventions and the author's proposed passage links. Record disagreements such as Nr.20 being placed on different walls in different corpus documents instead of choosing the location that fits a desired result.
2. Supply surveyed chamber geometry, horizon, light source, restoration alternatives and time-stamped surface observations. Retain the “base-contact” event as a required observation with an explicit geometric definition. Include dark intervals and relevant reader positions.
3. For each declared time and frame, produce the visible marks and all permitted two-channel candidates. Preserve uncertainty, side convention and alphabet. A transliteration candidate is not automatically a translation or an acoustic frequency.
4. Apply fixed, source-linked passage rules to those candidates. Produce the ordered passage sequence and its accumulated narrative context. Keep separate outputs for day, annual phase, cycle year and historical epoch.
5. Evaluate the predicted full sequence on held-out observation periods. Compare it with shifted times, rotated frames, swapped channels, shuffled passage order and alternative geometry. Apply the same search freedom to controls, preserve dependent data, and count the attempted choices. Repeated versions of one manuscript are one evidence lineage, not independent confirmations.
6. Scale computation after a measured pilot can reproduce light positions and the complete inference chain. Larger or longer frequency sweeps alone would not fill the missing observations. The temporal specification is ready for review and data completion; the historical timed-reading experiment has not been executed.
