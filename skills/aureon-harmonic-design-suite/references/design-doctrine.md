# Aureon public design doctrine

## Positioning

Aureon is a research-led evidence and control company with one shared core and several mission-specific applications. The site must communicate breadth without reading as a list of unrelated experiments.

Use this message hierarchy:

1. What Aureon is: an evidence, provenance, review, and human-authority operating layer.
2. What it enables: decision-ready research and controlled workflows across mission sectors.
3. How it earns trust: inspectable public evidence, sources, boundaries, and the next validation.
4. What research contributes: the proving ground, technical depth, and intellectual signal.
5. What a buyer, partner, researcher, or investor should do next.

## Competitor-informed blend

Use official-source observations as patterns, not parity claims. The current
evidence trail is recorded in
`docs/research/AUREON_INVESTOR_WEBSITE_PEER_BENCHMARK_20260726.md`.

- SRI International: separate core research capabilities from the sectors in which they create value.
- Anduril: make one shared platform spine legible beneath multiple domain applications.
- Anthropic: treat governance and transparency as first-class public content.
- Palantir: use a strong proof hierarchy and attributed impact only where the source supports it.
- Planet: caption technical imagery with provenance, scale, and limitation.
- IBM Research: connect research to inspectable tools, code, documentation, and artifacts.
- Google DeepMind: guide visitors from major questions into a searchable source record.
- Rocket Lab: own the category quickly, then explain the end-to-end capability and evidence.

Never copy competitor code, copy, imagery, visual identity, or trade dress.

## Visual language

- Tone: institutional, exact, calm, technical, and quietly ambitious.
- Typography: strong editorial hierarchy, readable body measure, and disciplined scale.
- Colour: deep neutral foundation, restrained spectral accents, accessible contrast.
- Layout: generous but not wasteful; hierarchy should remain clear at 360px and 200% zoom.
- Components: evidence cards, status rails, proof paths, tabs, packets, and source-linked records.
- Imagery: diagrams, research artefacts, technical editorial graphics, and source-cleared article artwork. Avoid generic AI-tech clichés.
- Density: enough substance to signal technical seriousness; use progressive disclosure rather than walls of copy.

## Motion doctrine

Every animation must have a job:

- orient the visitor;
- reveal hierarchy;
- explain a state transition;
- connect evidence to outcome;
- provide interaction feedback.

Default constraints:

- no ambient “system activity” theatre;
- no autoplay media;
- entrance motion at most 800ms;
- UI transitions normally 80–500ms;
- no infinite decorative loops;
- no motion that implies live telemetry when the evidence is static;
- `prefers-reduced-motion` removes nonessential transform and animation;
- keyboard, focus, resize, history, and pointer paths reach the same states.

## Copy doctrine

- Write in precise en-GB.
- Lead with the decision or outcome, then how it works, then proof and boundary.
- Make every CTA specific: inspect, read, compare, request, discuss, or download.
- Prefer exact states over inflated adjectives.
- Use short labels, clear route introductions, and consistent terminology.
- Separate “company-recorded”, “provider-confirmed”, “independently verified”, and “not yet evidenced”.
- Never turn an email acknowledgement, grant application, conversation, or submission into an award, customer, partner, validation, or financing claim.
- Make the public site an investor-attraction surface, not an operating ledger. Keep financial, route, correspondence, portal, operator, deployment, and hosting records outside the release.
- Prefer flagship research questions, methods, public sources, and next validation over corpus counts or KPI counters.

## Public-attention evidence

Use audience and repository analytics only when the platform, measurement
window, read-back date, source route, and interpretation boundary are explicit.

- Views show discoverability or reach.
- Downloads show record access, not reading quality, implementation, or use.
- Repository clones show technical access and may include automation or repeat
  activity; they are not verified users or production adoption.
- Stars and forks show public repository interest, not customers or commercial
  traction.
- Platform-generated mentions must be identity-checked before they are treated
  as citations, discussion, or independent attention.
- Never add audiences across platforms and call the result unique people.
- Treat each rolling or dated window as a standalone observation. Do not sum
  overlapping windows or add window-distinct visitors or cloners into a
  cross-window unique total.
- Join analytics across a repository rename only after preserving an
  authoritative redirect or rename receipt.
- Keep visitor-level exports outside the public repository and website root.
  Public pages may use reviewed aggregates and hashes, never visitor
  identifiers, location rows, referrers, keywords, or signed download URLs.
- Never convert platform analytics into scientific validation, customer use,
  revenue, independent adoption, or endorsement.
