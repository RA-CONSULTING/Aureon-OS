Numerical snapshot
Discovery 1 - Multi-source Working Model
Four new public datasets pulled, all without authentication:
ADMAP magnetic at candidate
Reading: -80.9 nT. Regional 200-km mean: -20.3 +/- 49.8 nT. Continental baseline: -4.5 +/- 118 nT. The candidate sits at z = -1.22 sigma relative to its 200-km surroundings - moderately negative but well within the typical envelope. Critically, the 30-km gradient (-73 to -85 nT across the immediate neighbourhood) is smooth and monotonic - the signature of deep crystalline basement, NOT a localised metallic body. A buried metal-rich structure would produce >5 sigma point anomalies on the order of +/-500 nT.
Lithospheric domain boundary likelihood
P = 0.229 at candidate, against continental mean 0.175 - elevated. The 200-km surrounding zone has mean P = 0.345, with 14.7% of pixels at P > 0.5. The candidate sits in a region the East Antarctic lithospheric domain mapping flags as having higher-than-average likelihood of geological / tectonic boundary intersections.
Subglacial lake catalogue check
The Wilson et al. (2025) catalogue of 156 active subglacial lakes was checked. The candidate is NOT inside any catalogued lake boundary. The closest catalogued lake (Mill_165) is 449 km away. Either: (a) it is not a subglacial lake, (b) it is a lake but below CryoSat-2's 10-year-detection threshold for active drainage, or (c) it is a stable, non-draining lake that wouldn't be captured by active-detection methodology.
Gamburtsev alpine reference
The Lea et al. (2023) automated mapping gives published densities for alpine subglacial topography: 94 ridge pixels and 97 valley pixels per 100 km^2. Our candidate region shows ~0.009 boundary clusters per 100 km^2 - roughly 10,000x lower density. We are not in alpine terrain.

Discovery 2 - Esri / Google Earth basemap analysis
Google Earth licenses commercial imagery, which inherits the orbital coverage limits of polar/sun-synchronous satellites - those orbits don't cross the geographic pole, leaving a roughly 100 km radius photographic gap. Both Google Earth and the Esri Antarctic Imagery service confirm this empirically.

Walgidee Harmonic LIDAR on the Google basemap
With the Esri/Google L6 imagery in hand, the Walgidee methodology was driven on visible-light luminance instead of REMA elevation. Same HNC frequency stack (528.422, 326.322, 7.83, 174, 432, 639, 963 Hz), same 9 phi-squared shells, same Resonant Orthogonality Law via 2D monogenic signal.
