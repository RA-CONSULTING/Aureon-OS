Executive Summary
This briefing traces a single investigative thread end-to-end: starting from observation of a geomagnetic storm event, and arriving at a multi-sensor consensus signature of a persistent physical feature in East Antarctica centred at −89.19° latitude, 110.65° longitude.
The path followed is deliberately laid out to show how each stage either supported, weakened, or refined the previous stage. Null results are retained. Positive results are retained. The final shape is what survives all tests.

Investigative Arc — Logic Flow
The investigation progressed through eight stages. Each stage posed a testable question, ran the data, and either sharpened or killed the previous conclusion. The sequence is presented as it unfolded.
Stage 1 · The Triggering Event
Starting point: a G1-class geomagnetic storm arrived 17–21 April 2026. Real event, multiple-agency confirmation:
This stage confirmed a real planetary-scale physical disturbance. The question became: does anything in the downstream data depart from what pure solar-terrestrial physics predicts?
Stage 2 · Cross-Rotation Comparison
A comparable event occurred 28 days earlier — one full solar rotation. March 2026 delivered a stronger G3 storm driven by a halo CME from AR4392. Direct comparison:
Figure 1. March 2026 (G3, halo CME, Bz −28 nT) vs April 2026 (G1–G2, CH HSS, Bz −4.2 nT). Both are textbook solar-terrestrial physics; neither shows unexplained features at the space-weather level.
Stage 3 · Anthropogenic Signature Test
Tested whether any human activity correlates with the April window: HAARP transmitter, VLF submarine communication, nuclear tests, coronagraph artefacts.
Figure 2. Anthropogenic evidence map. VLF belt-modification is real and peer-reviewed (Ma et al. Nature Comms 2020) but operates on decadal timescales. HAARP was NOT transmitting during the April window (last campaign Jan 28–Feb 2; next Aug 4–13). No nuclear or ionospheric activity in the window.
Stage 4 · Cloud Field Anomaly
Hypothesis reframed: the storm was natural, but did its propagation through the atmosphere look normal? Pulled MODIS Aqua cloud-fraction for ten days spanning the event.
Figure 3. Global cloud anomaly (storm window minus surrounding days). Red = more cloud, blue = less. Below the natural day-to-day weather noise floor (0.4σ). Pattern does not match the symmetric auroral-band signature that known geomagnetic-cloud coupling mechanisms (Svensmark, Tinsley) would predict.
Stage 5 · Persistent-Amid-Motion Detection
New test: find pixels that stay put while their neighbourhood moves with natural wind flow (stationary amid advection). Ran on storm window first, then a Feb 2026 control for sanity check.
Two candidates surfaced in Antarctica, but Feb control showed the same shape in the same place — indicating a topographic/cryospheric origin, not storm driven. Refined the framing:
Stage 6 · Full-Year Surveillance
122 daily MODIS Aqua cloud-fraction frames across April 2025 – April 2026. Sliding 5-frame windows; per-pixel flag frequency across the year.
Figure 4. Full-year persistence-amid-motion frequency map. Cyan circle = original candidate. Top panel shows global flag frequency; no pixel anywhere on Earth is flagged more than 20% of the year. Lower panel isolates pixels flagged >10% — reveals expected persistent features (Sahara, Arabian Peninsula, Antarctic zones).
Shape-tracking: 1,042 total shape appearances globally, 33 within 10° of the candidate. Centroid drifts systematically (~40° lon, ~10° lat span) following Antarctic seasonal cycle — not a fixed-position artefact, but not random either.
Figure 5. Antarctic candidate centroid position through the year. Systematic seasonal drift detected (p = 0.004 for longitude, p = 0.040 for latitude). Hue in lower panel = month of detection. Pattern tracks ice-edge/polar-night cycle.
Stage 7 · Harmonic LIDAR Application
Applied the HNC harmonic LIDAR framework developed for Walgidee Hills (aluminium-obsidian impedance matching, Project Druid). HNC frequency stack (528.422 Hz Lumina seed, 326.322 counter, 7.83 Schumann, solfeggio) translated to spatial wavelengths via ice acoustic velocity (3800 m/s).
Figure 6. Harmonic LIDAR Γ coherence over Antarctic ROI. Storm windows elevated in productive band (50%) vs control (20%). Fisher exact p = 0.042, odds ratio 4.09. Probe-contrast effect, not shape-generation.
