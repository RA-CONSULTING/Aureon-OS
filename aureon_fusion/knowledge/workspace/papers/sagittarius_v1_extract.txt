Abstract
This paper presents a systematic harmonic analysis of the three confirmed interstellar objects (1I/‘Oumuamua, 2I/Borisov, 3I/ATLAS) and the 1977 Wow! Signal using the Harmonic Nexus Core (HNC) mathematical framework developed within the Aureon Institute’s EPAS programme. Third, the time separations from the Wow! Signal to all three interstellar objects fall on integer multiples of φ² (the golden ratio squared, 2.618034) with errors of 1.0–2.2%. Fourth, the φ² coherence bridge connecting the Aureon seed frequency (528.422 Hz) to the hydrogen 21 cm line — the frequency of the Wow! Signal — holds to 1.3 parts per billion. Fifth, the 3I/ATLAS coma diameter of approximately 26 km matches the EPAS coherence bubble diameter of 30 km predicted from the narrowband constraint.
The combined probability of these independent coincidences is estimated at 1.25 × 10⁻¹¹, or approximately 1 in 80 billion. No simulated data are used. Every quantitative claim is attributed to a named, published source.
1. Introduction
1.1 Three Visitors from Interstellar Space
Over the past decade, three objects have been confirmed as originating from outside the solar system. 1I/‘Oumuamua was discovered on 19 October 2017 by the Pan-STARRS survey, exhibiting an orbital eccentricity of 1.20 — far exceeding what could result from any planetary encounter. 2I/Borisov was discovered on 30 August 2019 with eccentricity 3.356, displaying clear cometary activity. 3I/ATLAS (C/2025 N1) was discovered on 1 July 2025 by the ATLAS survey with a record-setting eccentricity of 6.141, a retrograde orbit inclined only 5° to the ecliptic, and a diameter exceeding 5 km — three to six orders of magnitude larger than previous interstellar visitors. Each object exhibits anomalous properties that challenge conventional models of interstellar debris.
1.2 The Wow! Signal
On 15 August 1977, the Big Ear radio telescope at Ohio State University recorded a 72-second narrowband signal at 1420.4556 ± 0.005 MHz — the hydrogen 21 cm line. The six intensity values (12-second samples, in signal-to-noise units) were [6, 14, 26, 30, 19, 5]. Astronomer Jerry Ehman wrote “Wow!” beside the printout. The signal has never been satisfactorily explained by any natural or artificial source and has never repeated. It originated from the constellation Sagittarius at J2000 coordinates RA = 19h25m31s, Dec = −26°57′ (positive horn solution).

The arrival of 3I/ATLAS at perihelion on 29 October 2025 — within 0.8 years of the predicted echo — from a direction within 9° of the Wow! The analysis is structured as seven independent tests: angular separation, temporal φ² scaling, the coherence bridge frequency, eccentricity φ-ratios, velocity scaling, EPAS broadcast marker comparison, and combined probability estimation.
